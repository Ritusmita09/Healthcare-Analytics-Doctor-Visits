Data limitations

Known data characteristics, measurement limitations, and analytical caveats for this dataset.

📚 This is an academic and portfolio EDA project. It is not intended for clinical use, policy decisions, or medical advice.
79.8% of respondents (4,141 of 5,190) report zero doctor visits.
This extreme zero-inflation depresses all group-level means, makes medians = 0 for almost every subgroup, and renders IQR-based outlier removal inappropriate (IQR = 0 because ≥75% of values = 0). No zero-visit records were removed.
Doctor visits range from 0 to 9 with mean ≈ 0.30 and strong positive skew (≈ 4). A small number of high-utilizer respondents drive the mean upward. The mean is not representative of a typical respondent.
illness, health, and reduced are self-reported — not clinically verified. Self-reporting introduces potential recall bias, social desirability bias, and inconsistency across respondents.
The health column (0–12) has no documented scale direction. Higher values may mean better or worse health. No directional interpretation is made anywhere in this dashboard. All health-score charts are labelled neutrally.
The reduced variable records days of reduced activity in a 2-week window (maximum = 14 days).
188 respondents (3.6%) have reduced = 14. This means "14 or more days" — true values beyond 14 are unknown (censored). These are not treated as outliers or removed.
79 respondents (1.5%) have income = 0.00. Without dataset metadata it is impossible to confirm whether 0 means genuinely zero household income or a missing-value code substituted with 0. No imputation was performed. These respondents are flagged with income_zero_flag = 1.
The dataset records gender as two categories only: female and male. Non-binary, gender-fluid, and other gender identities are not represented.
The dataset file does not contain metadata confirming country of origin, year, or survey methodology. The data is consistent with the Cameron & Trivedi (1986) Australian health survey, but this is not confirmed in the file. No findings are generalised to any specific country or population.
1,320 rows (25.4%) share identical values across all 12 original columns with at least one other row. This is expected given the limited variable cardinality (12 discrete age bands, 14 income levels, most other variables binary). These represent distinct respondents who happen to share attribute profiles. No rows were removed on this basis.
All findings in this project are associational, not causal.
A Spearman correlation or group mean difference does not establish that one variable causes a change in another. Observational survey data cannot establish causation without a randomised controlled design, controlling for all confounders, and temporal ordering of variables.

"Correlation indicates association. It does not establish causation."
